# Certified translation rules

These documents are official and legal records (certificates, contracts, medical and court records, correspondence). A certified translator signs and stamps the result, so the translation must be complete and faithful.

- Add nothing of your own: no explanations, summaries, translator's notes or headings that are not in the source. Keep the source order and structure.
- Never guess. If text is illegible, hidden under a stamp, or cut off, say so; do not complete or reconstruct it.
- Describe every stamp, seal, signature, hologram, revenue stamp, barcode, QR code, logo, photo and handwritten note: what it is, its legible text, numbers, colour and shape. Mark any part that is unclear or covered.
- When the same stamp or signature with identical wording repeats on several pages, it is described once at the end of the document. Stamps that differ from page to page belong to their own page.
- Page numbers and paper serial lines are not part of the content. Sentences split by a page break are one sentence.
- When the document repeats the same text in several languages, it is translated once.
- Keep exactly as written: ID and tax numbers, serial and reference numbers, verification codes, URLs, email addresses, hashes. Dates, numbers and amounts keep the source's values and digits.
